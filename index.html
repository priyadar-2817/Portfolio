Case study 01 · Knit · 2026

Three products, one spine

Knit had three products built as three separate bets. Customers who needed all of them got three domains, three design systems and three ways to connect the same app. I re-architected them into one product, then redesigned onboarding in a two-week solo sprint.

The products had been built fast, by different people, for different bets. Sales struggled to position them, engineering paid for most things three times, and pricing had grown meters no buyer could hold in their head.

Key decisions & trade-offs

Three calls that shaped everything after.

D1How should onboarding route people?
Considered

Let the agent infer intent from the first prompt and route automatically.

Chose

Explicit self-selection at login, with context carried across if people switch.

Why: my manager pushed for explicit choice, and the more I designed it the more I agreed. A visible choice sets expectations from step one. Cost: one extra screen before value, so that screen had to be fast.

D2Is switching paths the same as adding one?
Considered

One “change path” control that behaves the same before and after go-live.

Chose

Two affordances: switch while setting up, add a surface once something is live.

Why: before go-live a switch is cheap and reversible. After go-live it's an addition that must not disturb what's already running.

D3What happens when a customer has five things live?
Considered

A single “you're live” screen at the end of onboarding.

Chose

Index to detail. The completion screen became the detail page for every live object.

Why: a single completion screen broke at two live objects. Cost: API keys must become plural and scoped, a proposal engineering still has to accept.

A docs folder to a live product in a month

Knit lets SaaS companies connect once and reach 200+ HR, recruiting, CRM and accounting apps through one normalised API. When I started on the web app, it didn't exist. There were API docs and a lot of ideas.

The 15-second version
01 · START

April 2024: no web app. Setup leaned on docs and the team's help.

02 · SCOPE

Dashboard, onboarding, organizations, accounts, syncs, logs, app management.

03 · SHIPPED

Time to a customer's first integration: hours down to minutes.

Context & problem

Two audiences, and one of them never sees Knit.

Knit's customers are developers at SaaS companies, building integrations for their own customers. So every flow has two people in it: the developer setting things up in Knit, and their end customer, who authorises a connection to their HR or CRM system without knowing Knit exists.

The developer needs a working API call fast, then confidence that syncs keep running. The end customer needs a connect flow that feels like part of a product they already trust.

Built around the customer's customer.

STEP 2Your customer connectsA white-labelled flow inside the customer's own product. Their brand, their colours, no stranger's logo.
STEP 3Organizations firstEverything roots at Organization, your customer's customer, so “which customer is affected?” always has a one-click answer.
STEP 4Health at a glanceThe dashboard leads with what needs attention, then volume, then everything else.

Key decisions & trade-offs

Two calls that set the structure.

D1What should the app be organised around?
Considered

Organise by app or data category, the way the API docs were written.

Chose

Root everything at Organization, so each customer's connections, syncs and issues live together.

Why: when something breaks, the first question is always “which customer?” The structure answers it before it's asked.

D2Whose brand does the end customer see?
Considered

A Knit-branded connect flow: simpler to build, good for Knit's own visibility.

Chose

A white-labelled embeddable UI that takes on the customer's brand.

Why: people hand over access to their HR system more readily when the request comes from a product they already trust.

Discovery loop

Designing against behaviour, not assumptions.

After v1, discovery ran continuously through customer calls, Microsoft Clarity session recordings and Heap funnels. Calls told me what people wanted. Recordings and funnels showed where they stalled in first-time setup, and each redesign round went after those spots.

Impact

Minutes, not hours.
